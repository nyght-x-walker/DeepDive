CHAPTER 2 · PHASE 1

Where we draw the line.

In the last chapter we learnt where memory lives. In this chapter we draw the line we will defend later. We build one shelf of 20 boxes and mark three parts. Our buffer is 8 in blue, the flag is 4 in orange, and the second buffer is 8 in green. Our rule is simple. We stay inside our 8. Every copy here is capped, so it is safe to explore.

THE SHELF · 20 BOXES

Three parts with one boundary

Our shelf holds 20 boxes, offsets 00 to 19. Blue cells 00 to 07 are our buffer. Orange cells 08 to 11 are the flag. Green cells 12 to 19 are the second buffer. The dashed marker between 07 and 08 is our boundary. This is the same line we will meet again in the next chapters.

LAB · TRY IT YOURSELF

Type and watch where bytes land

Now try it. Type any text and watch where its bytes land. HELLO uses 5 boxes and fits inside our 8. The 15-letter input fills our 8, then 7 spill past the marker into the neighbours. Here the spill is only shown. Nothing is really written past anything, because every copy is capped.

WHAT THIS CHAPTER SHOWS

Length is not capacity

The point of this chapter is the difference between length and capacity. Length is how many bytes the input has. Capacity is how many the buffer holds. HELLO is 5 into 8, so it fits. The 15-letter input is 15 into 8, so 7 cross the marker. In the next chapter we remove the cap and see where those extra bytes really go.
